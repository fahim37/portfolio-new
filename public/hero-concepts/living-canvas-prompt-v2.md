# Living Canvas v2 — from detail to the whole system

## What changes

The reviewed export is 8 seconds. Its sampled frames show a gradual change of viewpoint over essentially the same woven fold. The previous prompt's modest camera travel and fixed surface produced an atmospheric background, but little visual payoff for a long scroll.

New direction: start at the same macro detail, then pull back dramatically to reveal that the surface belongs to one sculptural ribbon looping around a glowing core. The surprise comes from discovering the whole object. Retain one continuous camera move and a coherent object, rather than asking the generator to invent several transformations.

## Replacement video prompt

Create a 10-second continuous cinematic macro-to-wide reveal for a fullscreen portfolio hero. Use the supplied woven-surface image as the starting visual reference. The close-up silver weave is a small section of a much larger, stationary sculpture: ONE broad silver woven ribbon curving into an elegant open loop around a small warm-orange luminous sphere. A single orange filament is embedded along the ribbon's length. The entire sculpture already exists outside the opening frame; reveal it with camera movement, not by growing or morphing the material.

0–2 seconds: begin close to the silver threads and orange filament, maintaining the reference's rich charcoal shadows and recognizable woven detail. Immediately begin moving backward, so scrolling produces visible motion from the start.

2–7 seconds: perform a substantial smooth camera pullback and gently rise, revealing first the ribbon's thin outer edge, then its broad curve, then the open loop and the orange sphere inside it. The foreground texture recedes into one coherent sculptural object. Use genuine perspective change and strong parallax, not a flat image shrinking. Camera travel should be large enough that the final object is seen in full, unlike a subtle lateral pan. Keep the sculpture fixed and the camera aimed toward its center. Do not pass through the ribbon.

7–10 seconds: decelerate into a full three-quarter view of the complete floating ribbon and its core. The sculpture occupies about 65 percent of the frame height, primarily on the right. The left 40 percent remains a clean charcoal void for website typography. Hold the complete composition only for the final half-second.

Premium studio lighting: broad soft silver highlights, near-black graphite shadows, restrained warm-orange illumination from the core. Elegant smooth ribbon outline, fine woven detail only where it can be resolved, and moderate depth of field. Keep the orange strand attached to the same material points throughout. Stable exposure and fixed lighting. Every intermediate frame should remain legible when paused or viewed in reverse.

One shot. No cuts, fades, object morphing, spawning particles, extra ribbons, tangled threads, rapid spinning, camera shake, light flashes, text, or audio. Avoid dense tiny details in the final wide view. Landscape 16:9, 1080p or higher, 30 fps if available.

## Generation notes

- Replace the previous video prompt entirely: its instructions for modest travel conflict with this substantial reveal.
- If the tool supports a final reference frame, establish the complete ribbon-and-core composition as that frame first. An opening macro image alone cannot constrain the hidden whole object's geometry, so a text-only endpoint remains an uncertain generation target.
- If the generator only supports 8 seconds, use 0–1.5 seconds for detail, 1.5–5.5 for the pullback, and 5.5–8 for the complete-object reveal. Duration alone does not create a stronger scroll experience.
- Review the ending before upscaling: the full silhouette must be distinctly different from the first-frame texture, and the ribbon should not visibly morph during the pullback.
